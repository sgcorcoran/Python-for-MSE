**4. P-values Simplified**
- **Definition**: The p-value is the probability of observing your data (or more extreme results) if there is no real difference between groups
- **Interpretation**: 
  - p < 0.05: With less than 5% confidence we can say the data sets are the same. Therefore, it is statistically significant that the data sets are different. **Important**: We cannot say that we are 95% confident they are different because we tested whether they are the same - we didn't test to prove they are different.
  - p ≥ 0.05: At least 5% confidence that the two data sets could be the same (not statistically significant - no evidence of a real difference)
- **Warning**: A small p-value indicates statistical significance, but doesn't tell you about the size or practical importance of the difference
- **Example**: p = 0.001 means we have only 0.1% confidence that the two data sets are the same - very strong evidence of a real difference
